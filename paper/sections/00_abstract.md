# Abstract

Voluntary carbon markets suffer from a profound credibility deficit caused by opaque baseline calculations and uncoordinated cross-registry double-issuance. Existing digital Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (dMRV) systems rely on trusted cloud infrastructure, while blockchain registries simply tokenize unverified legacy paper certificates. We present VeriCarbon, a decentralized, zero-knowledge verifiable MRV architecture combining spaceborne remote sensing with on-chain global nullifier sets on Ethereum Layer 2 rollups. VeriCarbon compiles quantized gradient-boosted decision trees into Halo2/KZG SNARKs over the BN254 curve, proving that minted credits are bounded by satellite imagery without disclosing private land boundaries. Proving completes on a laptop CPU in 0.5504 s [C-04] with an invariant 4,032-byte proof [C-06]. On Base Sepolia, end-to-end issuance consumes 873,164 gas [C-09] (\$0.0119 at modeled rates [C-10]), dominated by in-circuit verification (643,269 gas [C-08]). Through formalization of Amendment A0, VeriCarbon derives salt-independent, registry-independent issuance nullifiers that provably guarantee parcel unicity. In adversarial testing across 9 security fraud scenarios [C-15], VeriCarbon intercepts all double-issuance and inflation attacks, whereas a conventional centralized baseline registry permits duplicate minting of 1,088 credits [C-16]. VeriCarbon demonstrates that cryptographically verifiable ecological accounting is practically achievable at negligible transaction cost.
