Dear Editors of Future Generation Computer Systems,

We submit our original research manuscript, "VeriCarbon: Zero-Knowledge Verifiable Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification for Decentralized Carbon Registries," for consideration as a regular research article. This work addresses the credibility deficit in voluntary carbon markets caused by uncoordinated cross-registry double-issuance and opaque verification intermediaries by unifying spaceborne Earth observation machine learning with on-chain cryptographic nullifiers on Ethereum Layer 2 rollups.

Future Generation Computer Systems is the ideal venue for this manuscript because our primary contributions center on distributed systems architecture, zero-knowledge verifiable computation, and decentralized consensus. Rather than presenting an econometric or energy-policy analysis, VeriCarbon delivers a systems-level computer science framework that enforces cross-ledger state synchronization and verifiable inference across decentralized, mutually distrusting standards bodies.

The manuscript presents four core contributions (C1–C4): (C1) we compile quantized gradient-boosted decision tree ensembles into Halo2/KZG SNARKs, verifying spaceborne biomass inference in 0.5504 s on a laptop CPU; (C2) we identify the salt-dodge double-issuance vulnerability and formulate Amendment A0, deriving salt-independent global nullifiers that provably guarantee parcel unicity and intercept all 9 evaluated fraud vectors on-chain; (C3) we benchmark Layer 2 execution on Base Sepolia, showing credit issuance consumes 873,164 gas (\$0.0119 at modeled rates)—a 4,585-fold reduction over Ethereum L1—with a p95 indexer synchronization lag of 31.57 ms; and (C4) we formulate a dual-commitment architecture balancing landholder boundary confidentiality with public auditability via in-circuit Poseidon hashing.

In accordance with FGCS artifact evaluation principles, the entire software pipeline is fully open-source, reproducible, and zero-cost (\$0 stack). Every benchmark and table in the manuscript can be regenerated deterministically via a single command: `uv run eval/run_all.py`.
